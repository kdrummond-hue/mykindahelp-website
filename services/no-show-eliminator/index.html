Frequently Asked Questions

What systems does this integrate with?
We support most appointment systems: Square, Google Calendar, Housecall Pro, Jobber, and custom systems. If your system has an API or export function, we can connect it. We'll confirm compatibility during the initial call.
How long does setup take?
Typically 3–7 days from kickoff to live. We need your customer data, appointment system access, and approval on the reminder templates. Setup includes testing, configuration, and a final walkthrough with your team.
Do I have to buy software licenses or pay monthly?
No. This is a one-time $599 fee. No contracts, no monthly subscriptions, no surprise charges. You own the automation once it's live.
What if my appointment system is old or basic?
We work with systems from paper scheduling up to enterprise platforms. If you export appointments to a spreadsheet, we can work with that. If your system is proprietary, we'll assess during the discovery call.
Can I customize the reminder messages?
Absolutely. We'll draft initial templates based on your shop's style, then you approve each one. You can customize tone, service calls-out, links to your website, whatever helps your customers remember.
What happens if something breaks after deployment?
You get a 30-day optimization window included in the $599. During that time, we monitor performance, tweak automation rules, and fix any issues. After that, you can reach out anytime for updates or adjustments (we'll discuss pricing for ongoing support separately).
How much will no-shows actually drop?
Most shops see 20–40% reduction in no-shows within the first month. Some see higher. The baseline matters — shops that already had some reminder process won't see as dramatic a lift as those starting from zero. We'll set realistic targets during the kickoff based on your current data.
Is this right for my shop size?
Yes, if you schedule appointments and no-shows hurt your revenue. Whether you're a 2-person tire shop or a 20-person repair facility, the math is the same: reminders work. Smaller shops often benefit most because labor is already stretched thin.
